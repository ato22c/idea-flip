### ③ Present your partner's work 🟡

**Steps**
1. The team lead takes 10 minutes before the meeting to pair all 10 people at random, making 5 pairs. Post the pairs in chat.
2. At the start of the meeting, each pair talks for 10 minutes. Each person interviews the other: "What did you do this week? What are you stuck on? Who do you need?" Both take notes.
3. After the interviews, each person has 90 seconds to explain their partner's work to the whole group. The talk covers what the partner did, what blocks them and who they need.
4. The partner then has 15 seconds to correct any mistake. The group does not discuss yet.
5. After all 10 short talks, the team lead spends 10 minutes asking: "Did anyone hear two pieces of work that look alike or touch each other?" Matching people agree on a next step right there.
6. The team lead writes the matches on the shared board before the meeting ends. Next week, draw new pairs so nobody has the same partner twice in a row.

**What you need**
- People: the same 10, with the team lead running the clock.
- Time: about 55 minutes in total (10 for interviews, 25 for talks and corrections, 10 for matching, 10 for setup and buffer). That leaves about 35 minutes of the 90-minute slot free.
- Money: 0. A timer and a shared board are enough.

**How to tell it works**
By the end of week 2, run a 3-minute check: each person writes down what 3 colleagues are working on. Count it as working if at least 7 of 10 get at least 2 of the 3 right. Take the same check in the week before you start, so you have a baseline to compare with.

**If it goes wrong**
If people look anxious or the talks turn into performances, shrink the talk to 45 seconds, let people read their notes, and let the partner say "I'd add this" instead of "you got it wrong." If that does not help after 2 weeks, switch to idea ① (a written sheet) and drop the spoken part.

**First 24 hours**
Today, the team lead writes the 5 random pairs for the next meeting and sends a two-line chat message: "Next meeting, you will interview your partner and explain their work in 90 seconds."
