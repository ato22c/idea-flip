**Start by moving the status updates out of the meeting and into a short written note, so the 90 minutes is used only for the places where people's work might collide. The meeting stays, but the part everyone finds boring disappears.**

🧩 The core conflict: the meeting has to tell everyone what everyone else is doing, but listening to ten people report is what makes it boring.

### 📋 At a glance

| Idea | One-line summary | Start |
|---|---|---|
| ① Three-line notes, then collision hunting | Read updates beforehand, spend meeting time on overlaps | 🟢 start tomorrow |
| ② Report on someone else's work | A teammate presents your work in one minute | 🟡 within this month |
| ③ Overlap huddles | Small groups meet by shared topic, then one line to all | 🟡 within this month |
| 🎲 Wildcard: Pop quiz opening | Random people say what a teammate is doing | 🔴 risky, test small |

---

### ① Three-line notes, then collision hunting 🟢
- 🛠️ What to do: Before the meeting, each person posts three lines in a shared place: what I did, what I start next, and what I need. Everyone reads them first. In the meeting, spend the first 15 minutes only on spotting two people about to do the same thing.
- 🎯 Why it fits: The meeting stays, as you required. It differs from the common answer "keep the same meeting but shorter" because the meeting's job changes from reporting to finding overlaps.
- ⚠️ Where it fails: People may not read the notes beforehand, and then the meeting just becomes reporting again.
- 🧪 First experiment: The whole team for two weeks. Continue if at least 8 of 10 people say they read the notes before the meeting and no duplicated work shows up.

---

### ② Report on someone else's work 🟡
- 🛠️ What to do: Each person is assigned a teammate's work for the week. They present it in one minute, and the owner only corrects mistakes. Switch pairs every week.
- 🎯 Why it fits: Nobody is added to the schedule and the meeting length stays the same or shrinks. It differs from "rotate the facilitator" because the listeners now have a job, so they must understand what others do.
- ⚠️ Where it fails: People may get defensive when others misdescribe their work, or may prepare only shallowly.
- 🧪 First experiment: Five pairs for three meetings. Continue if the owner has to correct fewer than 2 points per summary by the third week.

---

### ③ Overlap huddles 🟡
- 🛠️ What to do: Split the 90 minutes. For the first 40, three or four small groups meet in parallel, each made of people whose work touches. For the last 20, each group gives the whole team one sentence about what matters.
- 🎯 Why it fits: It is still one meeting, so no cancelling. It differs from "have two smaller meetings" because the groups are built from who overlaps, not from teams, and everyone still hears the key points.
- ⚠️ Where it fails: Overlaps may be unclear at first, and some work may sit between groups where nobody notices it.
- 🧪 First experiment: One meeting, with groups drawn from the three notes in idea ①. Continue if at least 7 of 10 people say they found something relevant to them.

---

### 🎲 Wildcard: Pop quiz opening
- 🛠️ What to do: The meeting starts with the facilitator drawing names. The drawn person has to say what a randomly chosen teammate is working on. Wrong or missing answers show who needs to hear what.
- 🎯 Why it fits: The meeting stays and it tests the real goal, that people know what others do. It differs from every common answer because the meeting is built around a test, not around information given out.
- ⚠️ Where it fails: People may feel put on the spot and become even less willing to attend.
- 🧪 First experiment: One meeting, five minutes only, with a friendly tone. Continue if people ask to do it again, and stop if more than 3 of 10 say it felt stressful.

---

### Next steps
- First check, as cheaply as possible, whether the boredom comes from hearing updates that do not concern each person, as I guess. Ask the team for a show of hands: for how many of the ten reports was the content useful to you?
- If the guess holds, try idea ① in the next meeting. It costs only the three-line note.
- If the result is thin, I can go deeper and sweep all the directions.

**Questions to ask the team**
- If we wanted to make this meeting as useless as possible, what would we do? What is the opposite of that?
- If the meeting could be only 30 minutes, what would we cut, and what would we keep?
- Which tool, chat or document do we already use every day that could carry the status updates?

### Reasoning
- Goal: everyone knows what others are doing, so no work is duplicated. Wider goal: the team makes fewer wasted moves.
- Constraints: the meeting cannot be cancelled, 10 people, weekly.
- Cause (a guess): the meeting shares all information with everyone, though most of it concerns only two or three people. If this is wrong and the boredom comes from poor facilitation, ideas ② and the wildcard fit better.
- ① separates the two jobs in time, so reading happens before and discussion happens in the meeting.
- ② separates by level, so listeners become reporters.
- ③ separates in space, so people meet only the part of the team that matters to them.
- The wildcard came from flipping the assumption that the meeting gives information, so it tests it instead.

### Appendix
- Safe answers I set aside: shorten the meeting, strict agenda and timer, standing meeting, rotating facilitator, shared task board, a written weekly summary, a chat channel for updates, splitting into two teams.
- Divergent ideas (technical term "principle" means a fixed prompt used to force new directions): [Preliminary action] written notes before the meeting; [Segmentation] huddles by overlap; [Inversion] listeners report on others; [Feedback] pop quiz opening; [Self-service] each person posts their own status; [Taking out] remove status from the meeting; [Another dimension] a board showing who works on what; [Intermediary] a note-taker who spots overlaps.
- Extremizing tools used: shrink the meeting to 0 minutes (what remains is collision spotting only) and flip the assumption that the meeting is for giving information.
